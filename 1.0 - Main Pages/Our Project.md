## Who we are

OpenBat is built by a few friends in Squamish, British Columbia, not a company or an institution. That shapes how the project runs: the scope stays honest about what a handful of volunteers can actually sustain, and rather than building new infrastructure from scratch, OpenBat plugs into programmes that already exist and already have the community's trust — the BC Community Bat Programs, NABat, and iNaturalist among them.

## What we're trying to do

Three things, roughly in the order a new visitor meets them. Bat acoustic data starts from close to empty in a lot of regions, so even a simple "there are bats here, tonight" record is a meaningful step forward on its own — it doesn't depend on getting the species right, mirroring iNaturalist's own research-grade standard.

## How we're doing it

A cheap handheld detector lets you hear a bat, but it can't tell you anything about it. OpenBat pairs live, on-device AutoID with the field guide, narrowed to what's actually flying near you, so the sound turns into a name, not just a noise in the dark. None of this needs expensive gear either — any standard USB ultrasonic mic works, including Phil Atkin's open-source [Griff Mini](https://www.pippyg.com/griff.html), buildable for around $20 CAD.

</div>

## How we're doing it

Most tools stop at recording — turning that recording into something a scientist can use is a separate, fiddly step that almost nobody finishes. OpenBat removes that friction: record on your phone, and get a properly labelled recording, spectrogram, and metadata, ready to submit to iNaturalist. Nobody has to "join" community science on purpose, the app just makes it the natural next step after listening.

## How we're doing it

The guide is expert-curated, not wiki-style open editing — contributions go through the [[Guide Editor|Field Guide Editor]] and are reviewed rather than published on sight. The species data itself is open too, CC BY 4.0, so the same trust standard that convinces professionals also means anyone can audit, reuse, or build on it.

## Not the first of its kind

The closest model isn't another bat organisation, it's Merlin Bird ID feeding eBird: a free app, run by Cornell Lab of Ornithology, that turns curiosity into records for a much bigger science platform, without being a conservation body itself. That's the shape OpenBat is going for — scoped to BC first, iOS first, built by a few people rather than an institution — but aiming at the same structural gap: not a new tier in bat conservation, but the thing that makes the tiers that already exist reachable by an ordinary person.
