Echolocation is easy to describe and hard to study. The describing is a
sentence: the bat calls, the echo returns, the delay is the distance. The
studying is decades of careful work on what the animal does with an echo that
arrives while it is still calling, in the middle of a swarm of other bats doing
the same thing.

Much of that work happened in flight rooms at institutes like the one at
Seewiesen — controlled space, high-speed cameras, microphone arrays, and a very
small number of bats doing one task at a time.

It is worth remembering when a detector prints a species name. The classifier
learned from recordings; the recordings were shaped by what the bat was doing;
and what the bat was doing was worked out here, one flight room at a time.
