Richter Clyde Perky bought a fishing camp on Sugarloaf Key in the 1920s and
found it unusable in mosquito season. The remedy he bought into was a
mail-order design for a bat tower: a tall wooden structure with internal
roosting slots, sold on the promise that a resident bat colony would eat the
problem.

The tower went up in 1929. The bats declined it. Accounts differ on the details
and improve with retelling, but the outcome is not in dispute — the tower stood
empty, a local landmark rather than a mosquito control device, until a hurricane
finally took it down in 2017.

The failure is instructive rather than funny. Build the box first and hope is
still the most common approach to bat housing, and the hit rate has not improved
as much as it should have.
