The tunnels at Nietoperek were built in the 1930s as part of a fortified line,
and they were never much use for that. Underground, at stable temperature and
humidity, with many entrances and kilometres of passage, they turned out to be
close to ideal as a hibernaculum.

Tens of thousands of bats of a dozen or so species winter there, and the site is
counted each year — which is what makes it valuable beyond its size. A single
large colony is a spectacle. A large colony counted the same way for decades is
a data series, and a data series is the only thing that can tell you whether a
population is going anywhere.

The management problem is human. Hibernating bats cannot afford to be woken, and
a disused fortification is exactly the sort of place people like to explore.
