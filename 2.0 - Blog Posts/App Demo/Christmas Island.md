The Christmas Island pipistrelle was a small insectivorous bat found nowhere
else. Through the 1980s it was common across the island. By the mid-2000s it was
restricted to a corner of it, and in August 2009 the last known individual was
recorded and then not recorded again.

The cause was never conclusively established — invasive species, introduced
predators and habitat change were all in the frame, and probably more than one
of them mattered. What is documented is the decline itself, because the species
was monitored the whole way down.

The uncomfortable part is the gap between knowing and acting. A proposal to take
the remaining animals into captivity was discussed while the population was
still findable, and the approval came too late to matter.
