The cave was known locally long before it was famous, and the reason anyone went
down it was commercial. Deep deposits of bat guano had built up under the
Brazilian free-tailed bat colony over thousands of years, and guano was a
valuable fertiliser. The mining came first; the tourism, and then the national
park, came afterwards.

The evening flight is still the thing most visitors come for, and it is one of
the few bat spectacles anywhere with a formal amphitheatre and a ranger talk
attached.

It is a useful reminder that bats were an industry before they were a
conservation concern, and that the colonies we now count were being shovelled
out within living memory of the people who started counting them.
