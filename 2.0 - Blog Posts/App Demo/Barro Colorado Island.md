Barro Colorado was a hill until the Panama Canal filled the valley around it. It
became a reserve in the 1920s and has been a working field station since, which
means a century of continuous observation on one patch of tropical forest.

For bat work, the draw is the sheer number of species sharing the same few
square kilometres, and the range of things they eat: insects taken in flight,
fruit, nectar, and — in the case of the fringe-lipped bat — frogs, located by
listening for the calling male rather than by echolocation.

A temperate detector tuned for a dozen insectivores has very little to say about
a place like this, which is the honest limit on any classifier trained where the
recordings were easy to get.
