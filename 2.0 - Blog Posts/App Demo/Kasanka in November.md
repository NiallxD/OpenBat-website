Kasanka is a small national park in northern Zambia, and for a few weeks around
November it holds what is usually described as the largest gathering of mammals
anywhere on earth: straw-coloured fruit bats, arriving from across central
Africa to feed on fruiting trees, and roosting in a patch of swamp forest of
only a few hectares.

The density is the thing. Branches break under the weight of roosting bats
during the day, and the evening departure fills the sky in every direction at
once rather than streaming out along a line.

Where the bats come from, and how they find the same few hectares each year, is
still not well answered. Tracking studies have followed individuals over long
distances, but the cue that assembles them is not settled.
