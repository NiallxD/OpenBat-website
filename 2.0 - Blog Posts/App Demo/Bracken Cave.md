Bracken Cave is a limestone sinkhole in the hill country north of San Antonio,
and from March to October it holds a maternity colony of Mexican free-tailed
bats large enough that the emergence is visible on weather radar.

The bats arrive from Mexico in spring, give birth in early summer, and leave
again in autumn. What makes the site famous is the exodus: it starts before
sunset and can run for hours, a column turning anticlockwise out of the mouth of
the cave before it straightens out and heads for the feeding grounds.

> [!note] Visiting
> The cave is owned and managed as a reserve. Emergence viewing is by
> arrangement rather than by turning up, and the closed season exists because a
> maternity colony disturbed in June is a colony of dead pups.

For a recording, the interesting part is not the column. It is the half hour
before it, when the first few hundred bats are audible and nothing is visible
at all.
