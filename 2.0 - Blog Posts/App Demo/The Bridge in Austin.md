When the Congress Avenue bridge over Lady Bird Lake was reconstructed at the end
of the 1970s, the new deck had expansion joints running the length of it. The
gaps were narrow, dry, warm, and well above the ground — which is, from a
Mexican free-tailed bat's point of view, a specification rather than an
accident.

A colony moved in within a few years, and the initial public reaction was what
public reactions to a large new bat colony usually are. The reversal is the
interesting part: the bats stayed, the health scare didn't materialise, and the
bridge turned into the thing people come to Austin in summer to see.

> [!tip] The transferable bit
> Nothing about the bridge was designed for bats. It is worth knowing what
> accidentally good roost hardware looks like, because it is cheap to repeat on
> purpose.
