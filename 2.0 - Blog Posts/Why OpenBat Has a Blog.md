## Where did it start?

During a research project to use passive acoustics to document bat species presence in the Squamish Estuary, our team built an audio processing pipeline around the NABat ML2 model in order to process the large volumes of audio being collected. At the same time, the project lead was tinkering with self-build, low cost, bat detectors and was curious to see if it was possible to integrate this open-source machine learning model into an iOS app for the purposes of suggesting IDs for bat species.

Many hours later and here we are! This has spiralled into a world of exciting and fun ideas, all which could end up improving awareness and enjoyment of bats for many other people, which is amazing!

## Why the blog?

Well, OpenBat is built in the open, it's literally in its name! But "open" mostly means the code is readable and inspectable to anyone, which is not the same as anything being explained. Along the way I have learned a tonne about bats, app development, digital signal processing, algorithms, APIs, and bats. I wanted to have space to share and document this.

The OpenBat blog is a place to dive into how the app functions, the nuances of bat echolocation, the challenges of working in this space, and successes and failures. It moves open from 'source available' to truly open and transparent.

In addition to this technical mumbo jumbo, other members of the team might want to share some stories that interested or inspired them, they live here too.

Thanks for being here! I hope you feel inspired to go learn some more about bats!

Niall & the OpenBat Team 🙂
