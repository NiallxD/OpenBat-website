To most people, all bats are bats. And that's not incorrect, but it is like calling all birds, birds. Can you imaging pointing to an eagle and someone shouting "LOOK, A BIRD!" it would be a bit odd, no?

There are actually lots of unique names we can use to talk about bats. Spread across 19 distinct bat families, each group of bats have their own group name that tells us something real about them! Think calling a bird with a 6 foot wingspan and a white head, an eagle. Or a small black bird with an orange bill, a blackbird.

## What's in a bat's name?

Below is a table of all 19 groups, but I wanted to highlight some which tell us a bit about the bats in that group.**Vesper bats** get their name from the Latin for evening, they hunt at dusk. **Horseshoe bats** have a noseleaf which is shaped exactly as you'd expect with a name like that! **Flying foxes** are large, with excellent eyesight and diet of delicious fruit. **Free-tailed bats** have a tail which protrudes out past the wing, which gives them an edge when it comes to speed. **False vampire bats** are really interesting, they are a bit like vampire bats, apart from they're not! They don't drink blood and instead they hunt other vertebrates.

Understanding, and using the common names for these groups of bat is super important to normalising them in our world, and offering some characteristics which are otherwise missed. When you see a bat next, I challenge you to try understand which kind of bat it might be, and then call it that 🦇
