"Get involved" is a useless sentence. Nobody has ever read it and known what to
do next.

So here's the specific version: the things that would genuinely help, roughly
ordered by how much difference they'd make, with an honest note on what each one
costs you.

## 1. Review field guide entries

**Who**: anyone who knows a regional bat fauna well — bat workers, ecologists,
long-time volunteers, postgrads.

**What it involves**: reading a proposed species entry and saying whether it's
right. Half an hour, occasionally.

**Why it's top of the list**: this is the actual bottleneck. Submissions can sit
waiting for someone qualified to look at them, and every week they sit is a
contributor learning that nothing happens when they help. More reviewers would
unblock the guide faster than anything else we could build.

## 2. Write entries for your own region

**Who**: anyone who knows their local species, including keen amateurs.

**What it involves**: the [[Adding a Bat to the Field Guide|guide editor]] on
this site — ordinary form fields, no code, no data files.

**Why**: [[Why We Started In British Columbia|the map has a lot of zeros on it]],
and each one gets filled by somebody local deciding to fill it. You don't have to
be a professional; the reviewer's job is to check, not to be impressed.

## 3. Photographs we're allowed to use

**Who**: anyone doing licensed bat work who has images.

**What it involves**: agreeing to license photos openly, with credit.

**Why**:
[[The Field Guide Photo Problem|good bat photos are rare, and legally usable ones rarer]].
A donated, correctly identified, openly licensed image is worth a lot and
costs the photographer nothing they were using.

## 4. Tell us about models we don't know about

**Who**: anyone following the acoustic classification literature.

**What it involves**: an email with a link.

**Why**: identification only works where an open, published model exists.
One message could turn a whole region from "detector" into "detector plus
identification", which is the single highest-leverage message we receive.

## 5. Identify other people's recordings

**Who**: anyone who's been doing this a season and can tell a bat from a
cricket.

**What it involves**: fifteen minutes on iNaturalist now and then.

**Why**:
[[Helping Identify Other Peoples Recordings|recordings pile up unreviewed]],
and an unreviewed record is half a record. The entry bar is much lower than
people assume — even confirming "yes, that's a bat" moves things forward.

## 6. Test the app somewhere unusual

**Who**: anyone with a microphone in a place, habitat or climate we've never
tried.

**What it involves**: use it, then tell us what was wrong or confusing.

**Why**: everything gets tested here, by us, on our gear, in our weather. The
failures that matter are the ones we can't reproduce — a microphone we've never
seen, a species that calls at an unexpected frequency, a phone that renegotiates
its sample rate.

Bug reports that begin "this probably isn't useful, but…" are almost always
useful.

## 7. Run a bat walk

**Who**: anyone with a detector and one evening.

**What it involves**: [[How To Run A Bat Walk|about two hours]] and a bit of
planning.

**Why**: public perception is one of the two structural problems in bat
conservation, and handing a stranger a detector is the most reliable fix anyone
has found for it. This is the item on the list with no technical requirement
whatsoever.

## 8. Android

**Who**: an Android developer who fancies it.

**What it involves**: rather a lot, honestly.

**Why**:
[[Why There Is No Android App Yet|there's no Android version and there isn't likely to be one from us soon]].
The models are open, the guide data is openly licensed, the formats are
standard. Someone building an Android client wouldn't be starting from zero,
and we'd help.

## What we don't need

- **Money.** There's nothing to spend it on. No servers, no staff, no marketing
  budget. [[Why OpenBat Is Free|the running costs are close to zero]] and that's
  deliberate.
- **Publicity for its own sake.** A thousand downloads by people who'll never
  get a microphone changes nothing. One local bat group adopting it changes
  quite a lot.
- **Feature ideas without a use case.** Not because ideas aren't welcome, but
  because "it would be cool if" is much less useful than "I was standing in a
  field and couldn't do X".

## How to actually start

Send a message via the [Contact](/contact/) page saying which of these you're
interested in. That's the whole process. There's no application, no onboarding,
and no expectation that you keep doing it.

If you're not sure which — say so, and mention where you live and what you know.
That's usually enough for us to suggest something.
