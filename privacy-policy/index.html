Privacy Policy

Last revised: 2026-07-26 · Corresponds to in-app consent version 2.0

The short version

OpenBat records bats. Everything it records stays on your phone unless you choose, recording by recording, to contribute one to the research project.

Recordings you contribute are anonymised on your phone before they are sent. They carry no identifier, a location rounded to roughly 100 metres, and a time rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. Human speech frequencies are filtered out irreversibly. Because nothing links a contributed recording back to you, we cannot identify which recordings are yours, and therefore cannot delete individual recordings on request. This is explained again before you contribute anything.

We do keep one piece of personal data: a record that your device agreed to these terms, so that you can withdraw. You can delete that record at any time.

Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA) applies to us directly. Because the app is offered to people in the UK and EU, UK and EU GDPR also apply to their data. Depending on where you live you may have additional rights under local law, for example under the California Consumer Privacy Act or Quebec’s Law 25, and we will honour those on request.

3. What stays on your phone

By default, nothing leaves your device.

Recordings you make are stored on your phone at full quality: exact location, exact time, and the complete audio including frequencies below the range bats call in. Species identification runs entirely on the device. Session tracks, the route and timings of a survey, are stored locally and are never uploaded.

If you have iCloud backup enabled, your recordings are backed up to your own iCloud account under Apple’s terms. We have no access to it.

These are your records. We never see them.

5. What happens when you contribute a recording

Nothing is ever uploaded automatically. There is no setting that would allow it. You tap to send one recording at a time, and that tap is the only thing that causes anything to leave your phone.

When you do, your phone builds a separate copy and sends that. Your original is never transmitted and never modified. Before the copy leaves the device:

  1. All identifiers are removed. No device identifier, no name, no original filename, no session label. There is no “recordist” field — the app does not have one.
  2. The location is rounded to a grid of roughly 100 metres. Everyone within the same grid square is recorded at the same coordinate.
  3. The time is rounded down to the nearest 5 minutes.
  4. Speech frequencies are filtered out by an irreversible high-pass filter. The filtering happens on your phone; the unfiltered audio is never sent.
  5. All other metadata is discarded. Only a short, fixed list of scientifically useful fields is kept — species, confidence, recording length, sample rate, microphone model and app version.
  6. A random identifier is generated for the stored file. It is not derived from anything, and your phone does not keep a copy of it.

The result is a file that cannot be connected to you, to your device, or to and other recording you have contributed.

What that means in practice

Because contributed recordings contain no personal data, they fall outside data protection law once uploaded. That has a consequence we want to be direct about:

We cannot delete a recording you have contributed, because we cannot find it.

This is not a refusal. There is no lookup, no restricted table, no internal process — the information needed to answer “which of these came from this person” does not exist anywhere. The trade-off is deliberate: the same property that stops anyone identifying you also stops us picking your contributions back out. You are told this before you contribute anything.

About the 100 metre rounding

How much protection 100 metres gives depends entirely on where you are.

In a town or suburb, a 100-metre square contains many buildings and many possible origins. In open countryside it may contain a single property, in which case the rounding conceals very little. If you are recording somewhere you would rather not have associated even approximately with a contributed recording, the time to decide is before you send it.

About other people

Most bat recording happens away from home — on walks, footpaths, and survey routes. That means the location attached to a contribution often describes somewhere you were passing rather than somewhere you live, and it may be close to a property belonging to someone who has not agreed to anything.

Two things follow. First, the speech filter protects those people as much as it protects you: a conversation indoors (why are you looking for bats indoors?) or in a garden you walk past does not survive it. Second, please apply the same judgement you would to any other recording made in a public place — and don’t contribute recordings made somewhere you shouldn’t have been.

6. What contributed recordings are used for

Contributed recordings are used to:

  • build a reference library of bat calls,
  • train and evaluate automatic species-identification models,
  • support conservation and ecological research.

They may be published as part of open datasets and may be licensed (sold) to commercial or research users, including ecological consultancies, to fund the project’s work. Because they contain no personal data, no personal data is included in anything published, trained on, or licensed.

They are never automatically published to a public live map or fed into any public-facing real-time feature. They are never used or sold for any purpose unrelated to bat research and conservation.

7. Withdrawing consent and erasing your record

To stop contributing: turn off contribution in Settings. Your consent record is updated to “withdrawn” and no further uploads are accepted. Recordings already on your phone are unaffected.

To erase your consent record: use “Erase My Consent Record” in Settings. This deletes the record from our servers and gives your device a new identifier, so nothing afterwards connects to it. It cannot be undone.

As explained in section 5, this does not — and cannot — remove recordings you have already contributed.

Erasure happens immediately. There is no queue, no review step and nothing that completes later — when the app confirms it, the record is already gone.

We keep a count of how many erasures we process each month. It contains no identifiers and nothing about who requested them, so there is no log of people who have asked to be forgotten.

8. Your rights

You have the right to know what personal data we hold about you, to have it corrected, and to have it deleted. Under UK and EU GDPR you additionally have rights to restrict or object to processing and to data portability.

Since the only personal data we hold is your consent record, in practice this means access to and deletion of that record — both available in the app, immediately, without contacting us or waiting for us to respond.

If you’re in Quebec, you additionally have the right to data portability — to receive the personal data you provided in a structured, commonly used technological format. Since the only data we hold is your consent record and it’s displayed in the app, you already have it, but ask and we’ll send it in whatever format is useful.

If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your data you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (priv.gc.ca). If you’re in Quebec you may complain to the Commission d’accès à l’information (cai.gouv.qc.ca). If you’re in the UK or EU you may complain to your own supervisory authority — in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk).

9. Who else is involved

We use Cloudflare, Inc. to host the service — the consent database, the recording storage and the API. Cloudflare processes data on our instructions under a data processing agreement, and does not use it for its own purposes.

Consent records are stored in Cloudflare’s Western North America region and may be processed in other countries, including the United States. Wherever they are held, they remain subject to this notice and to our agreement with Cloudflare, and may be accessible to the courts and authorities of that country.

Where personal data leaves the UK or EEA, the transfer is covered by Standard Contractual Clauses within our data processing agreement with Cloudflare.

We do not use advertising networks, analytics SDKs, crash reporting that identifies users, or any form of cross-app or cross-site tracking. The app contains no third-party trackers.

10. Privacy by default

Where OpenBat offers a choice that affects your privacy, the more private option is the default: contribution is off until you opt in.

Everything else is unconditional. Location generalisation, time rounding and speech filtering have no settings at all — they cannot be turned off, by you or by us. And there is no automatic upload, so nothing can be sent without you choosing to send it.

Contributing uses mobile or Wi-Fi data, depending on your connection at the time.

11. Children

OpenBat is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them.

12. Changes

If we change how any of this works, we will publish an updated notice and, where the change is material, ask you to agree again in the app. The consent version recorded against your device tells us which version you agreed to.

This notice describes OpenBat’s behaviour as of consent version 2.0.
